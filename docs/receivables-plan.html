Closing the gap between our receivables and a real set of books

OPS Fusion already runs the counter better than QuickBooks does — booklet serials, VAT backed out of every invoice, allocations that actually close a receivable. What it cannot yet do is prove itself against a ledger, or answer a question about any date but today. This is the plan to finish that — starting with the withholding-tax defect, which is now fixed.

Shipped first: withholding tax

This was the one active defect on the page. It is closed — and closing it turned up a second tax we were not accounting for at all.

Every peso a customer withholds used to become a receivable that could never close

A customer who is a withholding agent pays ₱98,000 against a ₱100,000 charge invoice and hands over a BIR Form 2307 for the ₱2,000 difference. That ₱2,000 is not a discount and not a bad debt — it is tax already remitted on our behalf, creditable against our own income tax. There was no field for it anywhere in the schema, so the open balance amount − amountPaid − settledAmount sat at ₱2,000 forever: inflated aging, inflated days-sales-outstanding, credit limit eaten, and no way for a cashier to clear it but to record a payment that never happened.

Then the harder half. Government, LGU and public-school customers — a real share of our work — withhold twice on the same invoice, on two different forms, claimed back on two different returns. Both rates apply to the VAT-exclusive base, and since 1 January 2021 the 5% VAT withheld is creditable rather than final, so it is money we get back rather than money gone.

AccountWithheldRateFormCredited against
1160Income tax1% goods / 2% services 2307Our income tax
1161VAT5% 2306Output VAT (2550M/Q)

On a ₱112,000 LGU job the base is ₱100,000, so ₱2,000 income tax and ₱5,000 VAT are withheld and the customer pays ₱105,000 — and the invoice must still read as fully settled. The two are kept apart on purpose: merged into one figure the receivable still closes, and the accountant is still left unable to split them across two returns.

The balancing identity in our own contract widened to admit both:

amount + creditApplied + ewtWithheld + vatWithheld = Σ allocations + creditCreated

Both rates are held per customer and auto-computed at the counter, with a cashier override for the job where the agency works it out differently. What is still outstanding is the certificate register — the 2307s and 2306s themselves, which are the paper we file to claim the credit back. That is now built too: certificates are recorded against the collections they cover, a certificate whose total disagrees with those collections is flagged rather than quietly corrected, and every withheld peso with no paper behind it sits on a chase list ordered by how long it has been waiting.

Where we stand

Built, verified by scripts/verify-sales-audit.ts, and staying exactly as it is. The ledger work goes underneath this, not through it.

  • Sales invoice ledger — VAT, Non-VAT, Charge and JO Slip, VAT backed out of a gross amount
  • BIR booklet serial control — all 50 leaves accountable, no gaps, no reissued numbers
  • Void / cancel / replace — spoiled receipts voided in place with a supervisor named
  • Collection receipts & allocations — no floating collections; every peso lands on an invoice
  • Customer credits — overpayments held as advance payments and drawn down later
  • A/R aging — bucketed, with statements and oldest-overdue tracking
  • Credit limits & terms — admin-gated, aggregated company-wide across contacts
  • Split tender — one row per method, dominant tender kept on the header
  • Withholding tax, both kinds — 2% income tax and 5% VAT auto-computed on the VAT-exclusive base, overridable, and the invoice closes
  • Certificate register — 2307s and 2306s on file, matched to the collections they cover, with a chase list of every withheld peso that has no paper behind it
  • Aging as at any past date — the ledger rewound, not filtered: debts settled since come back, invoices cancelled since return, and everything ages from the report date rather than from today
  • Sales report over any range — split VAT / Non-VAT / Charge / JO slip, grouped by day, week or month, broken down by customer, with collections reported beside revenue and never inside it
  • Excel export — three tabs, money written as numbers so the accountant’s own totals work
  • Unbilled & backlog — the three states of a job’s value kept apart and proved to partition it: on the floor, delivered-but-never-invoiced, and A/R proper
  • Downpayments, tagged and traceable — a job takes as many part-payments as the customer makes, each on its own serial, each booking the amount handed over, and the tag says on the day’s log who is coming back for the balance
  • Job order transaction history — every document ever raised against one job, in order, with what was still owed after each; cancelled ones kept and marked
  • Cash in, beside gross sales — what the drawer took and what the books earned, reported as the two different numbers they are
  • Integer-centavo arithmetic — money never touches a float, receipts always foot
  • Daily sales summary — split VAT / Non-VAT, collections excluded from revenue
  • Auditor sign-off — separation of duties between the cashier and the verifier

The plan

Ordered so that value lands early and nothing waits on the Ledger track longer than it has to. Phase 1 has no external dependency at all.

1

Close the receivable

Start now No dependencies

Make it possible for an invoice to reach zero the way it actually gets settled in practice — net of withholding, net of a credit note, net of a negotiated discount.

  • Withholding on the allocation
  • Second withholding: 5% VAT
  • Extended balancing identity
  • Aging & DSO corrected
  • 2307 / 2306 certificate register
  • Credit memo / sales return
  • Discount on the invoice
Done when An LGU invoice paid net of both 2% income tax and 5% VAT closes to a zero balance, the 2307 and 2306 are on file against it, and verify-sales-audit.ts asserts the widened identity holds. All but the credit memo and the discount are done — 45 assertions cover them, including that the withheld VAT is exactly 2.5× the withheld income tax, which can only hold if both hit the same base.
2

Reports that take a date range

Promoted No dependencies

Almost every report we have is locked to today or to a single day. Since OPS Fusion will not be a BIR-registered book, reporting is no longer competing with compliance work — it is the highest-value thing we can build, and it waits on nobody.

  • Aging as of any date
  • Sales over a date range
  • Statement of Account, any date
  • Customer payment ledger
  • Unbilled & backlog report
  • For Delivery report
  • Sales by customer
  • Excel export
Done when Every report above runs over a date the user picks, exports to Excel, and a real Statement of Account — opening balance, activity, closing balance — can be sent to a customer.

Collections

No dependencies

Handle the ways money actually arrives at a print shop — and the ways it fails to.

  • Milestone / instalment terms
  • Recurring invoices

4

Post to the books

Needs Ledger Phase A

Every sales event emits a balanced journal entry inside the same transaction that writes the receipt. Nothing already built changes — the ledger goes underneath it. This is where the job-order downpayment becomes a deposit and delivered-but-unbilled work becomes an asset.

  • Sale → journal entry
  • Collection → journal entry
  • JO slip → customer deposit
  • Delivered → unbilled receivable
  • Void → reversing entry
  • Branch dimension reserved
Done when A full cycle — downpayment, delivery, invoice, partial collection, withholding, void, credit note — leaves the trial balance balanced. That single assertion catches nearly everything else.
5

See the business

Needs Phase 4

The payoff phase. Once every peso is on the books with a customer and a job order attached to it, the questions the shop actually cares about become one query each.

  • DSO & collection efficiency
  • Credit exposure dashboard
  • Cashier shift close
  • VAT working papers
  • Zero-rated & exempt types
  • Senior Citizen / PWD discount
  • Per-job margin
Done when We can answer “which customers, products and jobs actually made money last quarter” in the app — something QuickBooks was never able to tell us.

Where the two tracks meet

The General Ledger is built by the Ledger & Payables track and consumed by this one. The interface is frozen in week one so neither side waits on the other.

The Chart of Accounts is ours, and it is already written

143 accounts, 118 of them postable, built for a Philippine printing shop — offset, digital, large format, newspaper, walk-in and merchandise revenue lines; VAT and withholding tax on both sides; work-in-process carrying a job order so per-job margin is answerable later. It ships as typed data in src/lib/chart-of-accounts.ts, with a validateChart() self-check that already caught one structural error in its own first draft.

The Payables developer seeds from it and posts against it. He does not design it, and he does not edit it to add an account he happens to need — he requests one, so the rollup tree and the statements stay in agreement.

They build

  • Journal entries & the posting engine — balanced, immutable, idempotent
  • Fiscal periods — so a filed quarter cannot be re-opened
  • Cash & bank accounts — where collections actually land
  • Trial balance & General Ledger reports — how both tracks verify their own work

We consume

  • We call post() and nothing else. No module writes a journal line directly.
  • Posting happens inside our transaction — receipt and entry commit together or not at all.
  • Our subledgers stay authoritative for operations; the ledger is authoritative for reporting.
  • Every void calls reverse(). Entries are corrected, never edited.

Decisions — settled 19 August 2026

These were the questions holding up the design. All but one are now answered, and they are recorded here so nobody re-opens them in code review.

OPS Fusion will not be BIR-registered

It is an internal management system. No CAS registration, no loose-leaf book formats, no return forms generated here. The IN invoice booklets are still registered documents, though — VAT arithmetic and serial accountability on that series still have to be right.

Settled · shrank Phase 4 into working papers

The NV booklets are not registered

So the whole non-VAT revenue branch posts as INTERNAL: it appears in the real P&L and never in anything the accountant sees. The VAT series posts to both.

Settled · reporting scope frozen

A JO Slip is an acknowledgement, not a sale

It books no revenue — the downpayment becomes a customer deposit, drawn down when the invoice is issued. The unpaid remainder shows on the customer's account from day one as backlog, becomes an unbilled receivable on delivery, and A/R when invoiced.

Settled · three-state model, Phase 4

Calendar fiscal year, cutover 1 January 2027

QuickBooks runs in parallel through Q1 2027 and is retired once the trial balances agree three months running. Since the system is not registered, a slipped cutover costs time and nothing else.

Settled · shapes the opening-balance load

Two branches, one ledger, switcher later

Tacloban is planned with a branch switcher — in Ormoc you see only Ormoc. That is a scoped view over one ledger, not separate books. Not being built yet; only the branchId column is reserved now, because adding it after fifty thousand entries means guessing which branch old rows belonged to.

Settled · column now, feature later

Still open: which QuickBooks reports are used?

Before building the statements we need to know which reports get run monthly, which go to the accountant, and which nobody has opened in a year. The QuickBooks account list also needs reconciling against our chart — anything that will not map is either an account we are missing or one no longer needed.

Owner + accountant · shapes Phases 2 & 5

How we will know each phase is finished

The house rule does not change: a phase is done when a green end-to-end verify script drives real services against a real database, and npm run check — typecheck, lint, and the sales contract scanner — is clean. Not when the screen looks right.

The contract scanner ratchets. Known pre-existing violations are recorded as counts and pass; anything beyond them fails. Debt can shrink, never grow. A fresh violation is never cleared by regenerating the baseline — it is fixed, or opted out in place with a written reason.

And one addition for this stretch of work: from Phase 3 onward, every verify script ends by asserting the trial balance balances. If the books do not foot, nothing above it is true.
